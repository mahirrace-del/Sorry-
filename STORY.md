# The story I want to tell you

*A first-person story for the game. It keeps uncertain dates uncertain and is written to remember our journey without asking you for forgiveness.*

## Before we really noticed each other

We were classmates before we became close. During the online-school years, new students joined our class, but I barely paid attention. I was the kind of boy who woke up, joined a class half-asleep, then went back to my own world. When school started feeling like school again, around the time we were moving through Class Seven and Class Eight, I was usually with my friends, joking around from the back benches and pretending I was too busy to notice the girls in class.

Then our teacher put us in the same science-project group. We had to build little houses to show the difference between series and parallel circuits. I liked the electrical part and was excited to make the circuits work. You were good at making things look lovely. You made tiny paper furniture for the houses, including a little sofa and bed, painted it, and sent me a picture on Messenger. That was the first time you messaged me directly. I noticed you through that small piece of work before I knew how much of my life would one day be made of little things you shared with me.

## A rooftop, a balcony, and a song

Around that time, you moved into a home close to mine. Your balcony could be seen from my rooftop. One day I was up there with my cousins, playing guitar and singing. You asked someone we both knew whether that was my home, then sent me a snap of the song. I sent one back, showing a little of where I was. It was a small, shy beginning: no grand confession, just two people finding a way to say, “I saw you.”

The messages grew from hi and hello into good mornings, good nights, and long stretches of talking about ordinary things. When the electricity went out and our phones went quiet, we sometimes went to the rooftop and balcony instead. We would catch each other's eye, match steps across the distance, and share songs without needing a proper conversation. I remember how simple and sweet that felt.

We went to coaching together and came home together. I tried silly things to make you notice me; you laughed at me, and I looked forward to that laugh. We sat near each other, teased each other, and sometimes skipped a class to go eat something. At the time, we called it friendship. We were becoming best friends so gradually that neither of us seemed to notice how much the friendship meant.

## The river and our very detailed plan

During a break between coaching classes, you suggested that we go somewhere. I planned the outing almost minute by minute: the nearby river, a boat, and enough time to watch the sunset before we had to get back. It was not called a date. We were still friends, and neither of us had worked out what our feelings meant.

We got on the boat and watched the sky change over the river. When we got off, the boat renter demanded much more money than we expected. I felt the situation could turn into a scene, so we paid what was demanded and left. On the way back in a rickshaw, there was so much we could have said, but we mostly looked at each other and let the silence do the talking. It was awkward, funny, a little unreal, and completely ours. Even the overcharge became part of the memory.

## Best friends, before we had the words

For months, our days filled up with messages and small updates. A good morning from you was often the first thing I looked for. I told you I had never had a girlfriend and that I thought no one would choose me. You told me to be patient. I used to explain love to you like I was some kind of love teacher, even though I had never been in love before. Whenever you said that someone would love me one day, a quiet part of me wished that person could be you.

We kept sharing the ordinary details: what we were doing, what made us laugh, what happened at coaching, and all the little things that would not mean much to anyone else. Those details became our own language. Our friendship was real, and the feelings inside it were taking their time to become clear.

## The silence, and finding our way back

There came a point when I convinced myself that people around us were not understanding our friendship in the right way. I decided we should make distance. I stopped talking properly at coaching, and when you asked what was wrong, I did not explain myself well. Later, I told you that we should keep some distance and end that close friendship. I said it as if I had already made peace with it. I had not.

We said we could at least say hi, but then we barely messaged. For the first time in a long time, two days passed without the way we normally talked. I felt how much I missed you, but missing you did not make my decision fair or clear. I should have spoken honestly instead of leaving you to guess what was happening.

I had kept the wrapper from a chocolate you had given me, framed it, and sent you a snap of it. You saw it and asked whether we could get back to how we were. I asked if you really wanted that. We started messaging again, unblocked each other on Messenger, and picked up our friendship with all the things we had been holding in. We came back as best friends, but with a better understanding of how much that bond mattered to both of us.

## The night our feelings finally had words

Neither of us can point to one official anniversary and say, “That was the exact day it began.” Sometime in November 2023, after my birthday on November 10, we were on a regular night call. We both seemed to know what the other wanted to say, but neither of us quite had the courage. Then, somewhere in that conversation, the words finally came: “I love you.”

I still like that the date stayed a little mysterious. Our story did not begin with a perfectly planned moment. It grew slowly through school, a paper sofa, songs across rooftops, coaching, a boat ride, messages, and a friendship we found our way back to.

## All the little chapters after that

Our years together are not only the big scenes. They are the messages, the photos, the letters, the calls, the jokes, and the small things we remembered for each other. I want to add only the memories that feel good and true for both of us. The pictures and letters in this game are meant to hold those moments gently, not make the past look perfect.

## What I am sorry for now

Recently, I said that we should break up, and I said, “I don't love you anymore.” Those were my words. Whatever was happening inside me does not excuse the hurt or the uncertainty they caused. I am sorry I spoke to you that way and made our relationship feel like something I could throw away in a painful moment.

I want to do better in ways you can actually see: pause before speaking when I am upset, explain what I am feeling without threatening the relationship, listen when you tell me I have hurt you, and respect the space or time you ask for. I know a promise in a game is only a sentence. The change has to happen in my choices after the game is closed.

You do not owe me forgiveness, an answer, or a decision because I made this. You can leave here whenever you want. I am sorry, Nidhi. Thank you for being part of so many chapters of my life. Whatever you need next, I will respect it.
